How to Write a Reggae Song: Structure, Hooks and the Moves That Work

How to write a reggae song: the structure, 90 BPM, line lengths, hook rules and the songwriting moves that make reggae work, with examples.

What a reggae song is made of

Offbeat skank (guitar chop on beats 2&4), one-drop drum (kick on beat 3), bass as melody, spiritual/political consciousness, community and resistance.

Reggae song structure

Long meditative verses. Chorus is comforting mantra, not explosive hook. Outro vamps are essential — let the groove breathe and fade slowly.

Line length: how many syllables

The fastest way to make a chorus feel like a chorus is to make it shorter than the verse. These are the syllable ranges SONIQ targets for reggae:

  • Verse lines: 8–12 syllables
  • Pre-chorus lines: 5–8 syllables
  • Chorus lines: 6–10 syllables
  • Title / hook line: 4–7 syllables
  • Bridge lines: 6–10 syllables

The rules of reggae songwriting

  • Bass is the lead instrument — it carries melody
  • Chorus is resolution, not climax — reggae builds peace not tension
  • Outro vamp is non-negotiable

How the hit techniques play in reggae

SONIQ writes every song with the Hit Craft method: 22 techniques that separate a hit from a draft. Here is how the first eleven sound in reggae:

  • Syncopation ratio: Vocal floats off the one-drop (~45%); chorus is a comforting mantra on the offbeat skank.
  • Density contrast: Conscious, fuller verse → a simple chanted chorus.
  • Shadow rhythms: The chorus rhythm is voiced first as a verse line over the skank.
  • Dopamine triggering: The dub break withholds the vocal, then the chorus returns with echo.
  • Rhythmic displacement: Chorus phrase re-entered on different offbeats in the outro vamp.
  • The woman in the room: Lovers Rock energy even in a conscious song — one verse addressed to her, tender.
  • Back phrasing: Laid-back, late, relaxed phrasing.
  • Micro-repetition: 'Jah, Jah', 'one, one', 'yeah yeah'.
  • Scene sequencing: The yard, the bus to town, the hills at dusk.
  • Repetition phrase types: Chanted chorus + vamp + call-and-response + bookend.
  • Juicy lines: A proverb-like line with a patois twist.

Structure and sound in reggae: Title as the chant's first line. Stable mantra chorus. Callbacks to the verse-1 place.

Reggae substyles

  • Roots Reggae: One-drop back phrasing; conscious scenes; chant reps.
  • Dancehall: Riddim syncopation 65%+; toasting density vs chant hook.
  • Ska: Upbeat syncopation; call-and-response horns.
  • Rocksteady: Slower back phrasing; romantic woman-in-room focus.
  • Dub: Sparse vocals, echoed micro-repetition; displacement via delay.
  • Lovers Rock: The woman in the room is the whole song; sweet back phrasing.

Songs that show how it's done

  • "No Woman No Cry" by Bob Marley: Anthemic simplicity — the hook is so simple it becomes a prayer through repetition
  • "Redemption Song" by Bob Marley: Message over groove — when the lyric matters enough, strip the production to nothing
  • "Rivers of Babylon" by The Melodians: Ancient words in new emotional context — the depth of source material resonates even without recognition

Songs that broke the rules and won

  • "Informer" — Snow: White Canadian rapper doing reggae-influenced pop in 1992 — the genre's cultural gatekeeping was absolute. #1 for 7 consecutive weeks, proved the groove transcends the performer's cultural origin.
  • "Electric Avenue" — Eddy Grant: Full electronic synthesizer production in a roots reggae context before that fusion had any precedent. Top 5 globally, proved reggae's rhythmic soul survived the digital production shift.
  • "Kingston Town" — UB40: British white band doing Jamaican roots reggae with pop crossover production — double cultural leap. Massive worldwide success for decades, proved authentic reverence for a genre trumps birthright.

The second voice: Rhythm guitar offbeat skank / melodica / horn fills

The rhythm guitar chop on beats 2 and 4 creates a persistent rhythmic counter-melody against the bass — together they form a locked two-voice counterpoint that IS reggae's groove. Melodica or horns fill the spaces between vocal phrases with melodic responses.

Where it goes: Throughout: guitar skank creates rhythmic counter / Verse gaps: melodica or horn fills / Dub break: bass and guitar in pure counterpoint

Ad-libs and vocables

yeah, jah, one love, irie, used outro vamp (non-negotiable), call-response in chorus. Outro vamp with vocable chant is structurally required in roots reggae

FAQ

What is the structure of a reggae song?

Long meditative verses. Chorus is comforting mantra, not explosive hook. Outro vamps are essential — let the groove breathe and fade slowly.

What tempo is reggae music?

Most reggae songs sit around 90 BPM. Offbeat skank (guitar chop on beats 2&4), one-drop drum (kick on beat 3), bass as melody, spiritual/political consciousness, community and resistance.

How long should reggae lyric lines be?

Verse lines usually run 8–12 syllables, chorus lines 6–10, and the title or hook line 4–7. Keeping the chorus clearly shorter than the verse is what makes it easy to sing back.

What makes a good reggae hook?

Chanted chorus + vamp + call-and-response + bookend. A proverb-like line with a patois twist.
