How to Write a Reggaeton Song: Structure, Hooks and the Moves That Work

How to write a reggaeton song: the structure, 90–95 BPM, line lengths, hook rules and the songwriting moves that make reggaeton work, with examples.

What a reggaeton song is made of

Dembow rhythm (syncopated kick-snare pattern), 808 bass, bilingual/Spanish lyrics, perreo culture, street credibility, romance and party energy. Production-first genre — the beat IS the identity.

Reggaeton song structure

Hook-first or hook-heavy. Short verses (8-12 bars), enormous hook repeated 4-6 times. Outro vamp is essential. Bridges rare — energy never drops for long.

Line length: how many syllables

The fastest way to make a chorus feel like a chorus is to make it shorter than the verse. These are the syllable ranges SONIQ targets for reggaeton:

  • Verse lines: 8–14 syllables
  • Pre-chorus lines: 6–9 syllables
  • Chorus lines: 6–10 syllables
  • Title / hook line: 4–7 syllables
  • Bridge lines: 6–10 syllables

The rules of reggaeton songwriting

  • Dembow pattern is non-negotiable — it IS reggaeton
  • Spanish lyrics (or Spanglish) dramatically increase authenticity
  • Hook must be the catchiest thing in the song by a wide margin
  • Production energy stays high throughout — no quiet moments
  • Specify "dembow, 90 BPM" in your style prompt for correct rhythm

How the hit techniques play in reggaeton

SONIQ writes every song with the Hit Craft method: 22 techniques that separate a hit from a draft. Here is how the first eleven sound in reggaeton:

  • Syncopation ratio: Dembow-locked flow, highly syncopated (60%+); hook syncopation stays high — it's the groove.
  • Density contrast: Dense verses → chantable hook.
  • Shadow rhythms: The hook's flow appears in verse 1 bar 1.
  • Dopamine triggering: Beat drop-out, then the perreo hook.
  • Rhythmic displacement: Hook shifted across dembow hits.
  • The woman in the room: Direct address to her on the dance floor — the hook she sings back.
  • Back phrasing: Lazy back phrasing on the romantic hook.
  • Micro-repetition: 'Dale, dale', 'mami, mami'.
  • Scene sequencing: The club, the car, the after-party.
  • Repetition phrase types: Exact hook + ad-libs + incremental.
  • Juicy lines: A flirty, bold line.

Structure and sound in reggaeton: Title in the hook's first line. Locked AAAA hook rhymes on the dembow vs slant verses. Call back a verse-1 line in the bridge.

Reggaeton substyles

  • Perreo Clásico: Dembow-locked chant hooks.
  • Trap Latino: Trap syncopation; dark scenes.
  • Reggaeton Romántico: Romantic hooks to her.
  • Urbano Latino: Melodic rap hooks.
  • Dembow Puro: Fast dembow chant.
  • Reggaeton Pop: Pop hooks on dembow.

Songs that show how it's done

  • "Gasolina" by Daddy Yankee: Dembow as identity — the rhythm IS the genre signature, hook rides it rather than fighting it
  • "Con Calma" by Daddy Yankee & Snow: Sample as cultural memory — familiar rhythm triggers nostalgia while new hook feels immediate
  • "Hawái" by Maluma: Romantic vulnerability over hard beat — soft words on a hard rhythm amplifies both

Songs that broke the rules and won

  • "Gasolina" — Daddy Yankee: Crude unpolished production and explicit content with no romantic veneer — against genre's romantic tradition. Launched reggaeton globally, proved the rawer the better when the dembow is undeniable.
  • "Tití Me Preguntó" — Bad Bunny: Ironic self-deprecating comedy in a genre known for machismo and seriousness. One of 2022's biggest global hits, showed reggaeton could be funny and still dominate.
  • "Con Calma" — Daddy Yankee ft. Snow: Recycled Snow's 1992 "Informer" hook — broke the originality-first production rule with nostalgia. Topped charts globally, proved strategic nostalgia beats chasing current trends.

The second voice: Dembow rhythmic counter / producer synth melody

The dembow kick-snare pattern creates a persistent rhythmic counterpoint against the melodic hook — the syncopated "and" hit is the counter-accent that defines reggaeton's groove. A producer synth melody or vocal chop often plays a secondary melodic line under the main hook in the perreo section.

Where it goes: Throughout: dembow creates rhythmic counter / Hook: producer melody layers under vocal / Perreo section: synth counter-melody emerges / Outro: counter-melody leads fade

Ad-libs and vocables

aye, eh, yo, dale, used perreo section hook, outro vamp, dembow accent. "Dale" (Daddy Yankee signature) is the genre-defining vocable — signals authentic reggaeton culture

FAQ

What is the structure of a reggaeton song?

Hook-first or hook-heavy. Short verses (8-12 bars), enormous hook repeated 4-6 times. Outro vamp is essential. Bridges rare — energy never drops for long.

What tempo is reggaeton music?

Most reggaeton songs sit around 90–95 BPM. Dembow rhythm (syncopated kick-snare pattern), 808 bass, bilingual/Spanish lyrics, perreo culture, street credibility, romance and party energy.

How long should reggaeton lyric lines be?

Verse lines usually run 8–14 syllables, chorus lines 6–10, and the title or hook line 4–7. Keeping the chorus clearly shorter than the verse is what makes it easy to sing back.

What makes a good reggaeton hook?

Exact hook + ad-libs + incremental. A flirty, bold line.
