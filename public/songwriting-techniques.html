The 22 techniques

  1. Syncopation ratio. How many stressed words land off the beat. Verses run higher, the chorus anchors on the beat so it is easy to sing.
  2. Density contrast. Chorus lines clearly shorter than verse lines, so the hook feels like a release.
  3. Shadow rhythms. A line's rhythm repeats with new words; the hook's rhythm is planted early in verse 1.
  4. Dopamine triggering. Set up an expected word, hold it a beat longer, then pay it off. One surprise per section.
  5. Rhythmic displacement. The same phrase returns starting on a different beat, so the repeat sounds new.
  6. The woman in the room. One hook line sung straight to a specific listener: the line people caption and send.
  7. Back phrasing. Lines that enter late, after the downbeat, and spill into the next bar for lean-in tension.
  8. Micro-repetition. A word or short fragment repeated inside a line ("gone, gone").
  9. Scene sequencing. Each section is one camera shot; verse 2 moves to a new place or time.
  10. Repetition phrase types. At least three kinds of repetition: exact hook, one word changed, verse tag, call-and-response, bookend.
  11. Juicy lines. Every section carries one line worth quoting on its own, verse 1 included.
  12. Prosody. Stressed syllables on strong beats, and a line shape that matches its meaning.
  13. Rhyme-type ladder. Slant rhymes for tension in verses, perfect rhyme saved for the payoff.
  14. Stable vs unstable sections. Verses feel unfinished; the chorus feels like arriving home.
  15. Line-length asymmetry. A short line after long ones hits like a punch.
  16. Callbacks. A verse-1 image returns in the bridge or final verse with new weight.
  17. Rhyme-scheme contrast. Each section type uses a different rhyme pattern.
  18. Title placement. The title opens or closes the chorus, on its most stressed beat.
  19. Open loops. Verse 1 raises a question the ending answers.
  20. Boxes. The same chorus means something new each time the verses change its context.
  21. Pronoun ambiguity. One "you" that can be heard two ways: a lover, a parent, God, a younger self.
  22. Tense and time travel. Sections move between past, present and future on purpose.
